The creators behind the feeling

Different disciplines. Shared emotions.

Meet a painter who transformed color into movement and a composer whose melodies continue to inspire listeners around the world.

Visual art

Vincent van Gogh

1853–1890 · Post-Impressionism

Vincent van Gogh is known for expressive brushwork, vivid color, and paintings that feel full of energy. His art often encourages viewers to notice not only what is depicted, but also the emotion created by the way it is painted.

In works such as The Starry Night, swirling forms and contrasts of light and dark can suggest motion. A viewer might connect that movement with a flowing melody or a piece that gradually builds in intensity.

Listen with your eyes: Notice the rhythm of the brushstrokes. Do they feel calm, restless, or dramatic to you?

Classical music

Ludwig van Beethoven

1770–1827 · Composer

Beethoven's music is celebrated for its emotional range, memorable themes, and powerful contrasts. His compositions can move from quiet reflection to energetic intensity, creating a journey for the listener.

Für Elise is one of his best-known piano pieces. Its recognizable opening can feel playful, delicate, nostalgic, or mysterious depending on the listener's experiences and interpretation.

Paint with your ears: If this melody were a color, would you choose warm gold, deep blue, or something unexpected?

A shared creative language

How painting and music connect

◉

Color & tone

Color creates visual atmosphere, while musical tone shapes the atmosphere we hear.

〰

Brushstroke & rhythm

Repeating shapes and brushstrokes can feel rhythmic, much like repeating beats or musical phrases.

✳

Contrast & dynamics

Light and dark can create visual drama, just as soft and loud passages create musical contrast.

These connections are invitations to interpret art, not universal rules. People may experience the same artwork or musical piece differently.
